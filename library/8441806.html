The Division Bell

Production notes

Originally released in March 1994, The Division Bell is the fourteenth studio album by Pink Floyd and the last to feature keyboardist Richard Wright, who died in 2008. A largely David Gilmour–led work—co-produced by Gilmour and Bob Ezrin—much of it was recorded aboard Gilmour’s houseboat studio, the Astoria, moored on the Thames, with additional sessions at Britannia Row, Metropolis, and The Creek. The album reunited the band with longtime collaborators including engineer Andy Jackson, saxophonist Dick Parry, and the late orchestral arranger Michael Kamen. It topped charts on both sides of the Atlantic and produced the instrumental “Marooned,” which won a Grammy, alongside the closing anthem “High Hopes.” Storm Thorgerson’s iconic cover—two giant metal heads facing each other across a Cambridgeshire field—remains one of the band’s most recognizable images.

About this 2016 European repress (2×LP, 180g gatefold): Parlophone / Pink Floyd Records catalog PFRLP14, barcode 0825646293285. Discogs release [r8441806], part of the Pink Floyd Records vinyl reissue campaign (the same PFRLP series as your Animals copy). Made in the E.U., pressed by Optimal Media GmbH in Germany, issued in a gatefold sleeve with printed inner sleeves.

The remaster provenance is worth stating precisely (per the credits on the release, not by assumption): the album was remastered from the original analogue tapes by James Guthrie, Joel Plante, and Doug Sax, with the vinyl mastering by Doug Sax and lacquers cut at The Mastering Lab. Your deadwax bears this out—every side carries the machine-stamped TML-M (The Mastering Lab) alongside the hand-etched matrix 2564629328-[A/B/C/D] RE… BE49494-…. This is a Mastering Lab cut, not one of the Bernie Grundman “BG” cuts found on the recent Animals and Dark Side reissues; the distinction matters for how these pressings are compared. The PFRLP14 etching on the labels and in the runout is what separates this 2016 repress from the visually similar 2014 US (RP1-543752) and 2014 European first pressings that share the same barcode.
